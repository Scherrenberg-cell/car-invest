Condition Scale — Standard Definitions

Five-point grading scale aligned with Hagerty Price Guide, RM Sotheby's and Bonhams auction grading standards. Condition affects estimated value through a tier-specific non-linear matrix — the premium between Perfect and Driver condition is far larger for trophy assets than for common classics.

ScaleCondition TierDefinition
(1) Concours / Perfect Flawless; professional museum quality; no mechanical or cosmetic defects; zero signs of wear. Trailer or enclosed-transport only. Commands the highest auction premiums.
(2) Excellent / Fine Exceptionally clean, well-maintained original or high-end older restoration. Wins local concours shows. Near-flawless paint and interior; mechanically excellent.
(3) Very Good / Driver ★ The "20-footer" — presentable from a distance, mechanically sound, shows minor wear or light patina. No immediate work needed. Reference condition for all base valuations. Most transactions at auction fall in this grade.
(4) Good / Usable Drivable but needs cosmetic or minor mechanical work. An older amateur restoration or tired original. Good "project base" — value reflects work needed. Does not command a premium.
(5) Poor / Restorable Complete but significantly degraded. Needs total restoration of body, interior, or engine. Not roadworthy but not a parts car. Priced near parts/restoration cost.

Investment Score — Five categories

The Investment Score (1–10) measures the intrinsic investment quality of a vehicle based on appreciation potential, rarity, brand prestige, maintenance burden and liquidity. It does not include country tax or personal usage costs. Formula: S = 0.40×O + 0.30×Fprov + 0.15×B − 0.05×G − 0.10×Lrisk + 2

Operational Score — Five categories

The Operational Score (1–10) measures how attractive a car is when you also use it — combining financial net return after all costs and tax with day-to-day usability. Formula: OA = 0.50 × Financial Score + 0.50 × Ease Score

Financial Score (1–10): after-tax net return scaled — 0% = 5.0, +8% = 9.0, −8% = 1.0
Ease Score (1–10): 10 − (Mechanical Complexity + Maintenance Risk) / 2

About the tool

The Carnomics Investment Estimator covers 1,340 vehicles across 63 marques (€3,490–€48 M). It produces a condition- and mileage-adjusted market value, a three-scenario projected net return, and a composite Investment Score for every vehicle — all calibrated against real auction data from RM Sotheby's, Bonhams, Hagerty and Classic.com.

Scoring revised June 2026, grounded in hedonic pricing literature (Le Fur 2023, Knight Frank KFLII 2026, Masset & Weisskopf 2018).

Operational Score — Methodology

The Operational Score measures how attractive a vehicle is when also used for driving — not just held as an investment. It combines two equally-weighted components:

OA = 0.50 × Financial Score + 0.50 × Ease Score
ComponentFormulaSource data
Financial ScoreMIN(10, MAX(1, 5 + (after-tax net return / 0.08) × 4))Condition & country-adjusted net return after appreciation, ownership cost and tax drag
Ease ScoreMIN(10, MAX(1, 10 − (Mechanical Complexity + Maintenance Risk) / 2))Model-specific mechanical complexity and maintenance risk from the database

The Ease Score penalises complex, difficult-to-maintain vehicles — a Ferrari V12 or a hand-built British GT scores near 1–2 on ease, correctly flagging it as "Collection only" for operational purposes even if financially attractive.

Key assumptions
  • Base values represent a typical Good-condition market price cross-referenced against real auction records. They are mid-market estimates, not asking prices.
  • Appreciation rates are brand-level benchmarks calibrated on Hagerty Price Guide 5-year CAGR data and Classic.com 10-year trend indices.
  • 50 Trophy-Asset CAGR overrides are applied for ultra-rare vehicles (Ferrari 250 GTO, Aston DB4 GT Zagato, etc.) where individually researched rates replace the formula.
  • Ownership costs range 2.5–8.5% of value per annum. They do not vary with individual restoration state or storage location within this model.
  • Brand Prestige scores (new in v6.3) are point estimates derived from KFLII 2026 and auction sell-through data.
  • Tax schedules reflect published 2025/2026 rates. All three country regimes are subject to legislative change.
⚠ Potential pitfalls
  • Not an appraisal. All outputs are directional planning estimates. Individual auction results vary enormously based on specification, provenance documentation and vendor reputation.
  • Condition × mileage interaction. Applying Concours and very low mileage together may overstate values for pristine survivors — the factors are independent but correlated in reality.
  • Thin markets. Rare cars (rarity ≥ 8) trade infrequently; one distressed sale can move apparent market value by 30–40%.
  • Pre-war penalty. The revised era modifier (−0.3pp for pre-1950 cars) reflects current generational demand research.
  • No momentum guarantee. Bull/Bear momentum flags are qualitative assessments and may lag actual price movements by 12–18 months.
  • Currency risk. Base values are in EUR. Auction markets are USD and GBP dominated.
Restoration Break-Even — Methodology

The Restoration Break-Even page answers one practical question: what is the maximum budget you can invest in restoring a specific car before the restoration cost exceeds the resulting market value gain?

Break-Even = BaseValue × CF(after) × MF − BaseValue × CF(before) × MF
VariableDescription
CF(before / after)Condition Factor at the respective condition input (1.0–5.0), interpolated from the tier-specific CONDITION_MATRIX
MFMileage Factor from the Hagerty-calibrated curve anchored at 80,000 km = 1.000
BaseValueReference market value at Very Good / 80,000 km from the database

The break-even is the value delta, not the restoration profit. Spending exactly the break-even amount means the market value of the restored car equals the total cost (purchase + restoration). Spending less creates an immediate gain; spending more requires future appreciation to recover the overrun.

Restoration cost estimate uses: BaseValue × (0.03 + (MechComplexity + MaintRisk)/2 × 0.012) × condition delta, scaled ×0.65–×1.55 for low/high range. Annual appreciation uplift = condApprecAdj(after) − condApprecAdj(before), applied to the post-restoration value. Payback = overrun above break-even ÷ annual appreciation uplift in EUR.
